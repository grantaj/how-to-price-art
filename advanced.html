Advanced sales optimiser

An experimental scenario model for production, demand and sales. For a straightforward artwork price estimate, use the main calculator.

An artist could make a few large, expensive works or many smaller, more accessible ones. Which approach sells more, and which earns more? This calculator starts with published Australian auction results and gallery floor sheets, and researched artist career histories. You control the unmeasured demand assumptions. It is a model to question, not a machine that knows what your art is worth.

Where the price numbers come from

For each candidate size, we find the five closest observed works by log area within the selected market and career stage. Their median price anchors the model. Auction anchors use reported sale results; gallery anchors use advertised asking prices (even when labelled sold).

What is still hypothetical

Potential purchases follow D ~ Poisson(λ), where λ = buyers × market strength × size appeal × (price / adjusted price anchor)^(-elasticity). Expected sales = E[min(works made, D)].

Cash contribution = price × expected sales × (1 − commission) − production costs.

The model optimises a single square artwork size for a period. Career stage filters documented observations; it does not set a fictional career-stage markup. Buyer counts and price elasticity cannot be estimated from prices alone.

Limits: Published prices anchor the model, but buyers, price sensitivity, preferences, production cost and time assumptions remain hypothetical. Cash contribution excludes your labour cost, tax, framing, shipping, rent, promotion and unsold-inventory storage. The optimum is conditional on assumptions—not pricing advice.
