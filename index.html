The Art of Selling

They don't teach you this at art school. How large should you paint, what should you charge, and how many works should you make?

An artist could make a few large, expensive works or many smaller, more accessible ones. Which approach sells more, and which earns more? This calculator explores how career stage, buyers, market conditions and studio capacity interact. It is a model to question, not a machine that knows what your art is worth.

How it works

Potential purchases are random: D ~ Poisson(λ). Price changes demand according to a constant-elasticity curve relative to the benchmark for each size. Size preference and market conditions also change λ.

Expected sales = E[min(works made, D)]

Cash contribution = price × expected sales × (1 − commission) − production costs

A size-adjusted benchmark price grows approximately with the artwork's linear dimensions. Materials grow with area; labour hours grow less than linearly with area. We search eight square sizes (20–120 cm), prices from 60% to 230% of each size's benchmark and all feasible quantities given studio capacity.

This is one product type, one price per work and one annual selling period. It does not model a mixture of sizes, collector loyalty, price signalling, exhibitions, changing career reputation or future resale value. Those would be useful extensions.

Limits: All starting prices, demand counts, preferences, cost/time formulas and price elasticities are deliberately hypothetical, not inferred from surveys or sales data. Cash contribution excludes your labour cost, tax, framing, shipping, rent, promotion and unsold-inventory storage. The optimum is conditional on assumptions—not pricing advice.
